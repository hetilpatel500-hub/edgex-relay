## The honest ground rule first

"No human at all" isn't possible for getting paid. Every platform that
sends money out (Stripe, Etsy, Amazon KDP, Fiverr, YouTube AdSense, etc.)
requires a real person's ID, bank account, and tax info. Agents can't do
that verification. What agents *can* do is almost all of the work after
that: research, build, write, design, sell, deliver, and support. So
"minimal human" here means **the owner sets up each payout account once,
and agents run everything after that.**

## Not on this list, on purpose

- **Trading.** The owner keeps it separate from this studio.
- **Fake reviews, fake engagement, bought followers.** This is fraud and gets accounts banned.
- **Mass-produced AI SEO spam sites.** Google's scaled-content-abuse policy demotes them.
- **AI voiceover gigs sold as human voice work.** This deceives the buyer and violates platform rules.
- **Dropshipping and domain flipping.** Both need upfront capital, and this studio spends nothing without the owner's budget approval.

## How agents work the list

**Not all 100 at once.** Spreading 110 agents across 100 ideas means
nothing ships. It's the same failure as the single-niche problem in
`OPERATIONS.md`, just in the other direction. Instead:

1. **Triage (about the next 10 hourly shifts).** Opportunity Discovery
   (Market Signal, Standing Opportunity Scout, Idea Validation) reviews
   about 10 `money_map` items per shift. Each item gets real demand
   evidence, real pricing, and a fit check against the studio's actual
   tools. The result is one of:
   - **promote**: a new `opportunities` doc at `validating`
   - **hold**: blocked on a specific `[SETUP]` item; name which one
   - **archive**: dropped, with the reason

   Every result is written back to that item's `money_map` doc.
2. **Active cap: 6–8 opportunities at a time.** They're ranked by the
   fastest real path to a first dollar with the tools that exist today,
   not by how big the idea sounds.
3. **Rotation.** When an active opportunity closes a deal, stalls, or gets
   archived, the next best promoted item takes its slot.
4. **Every client-facing or public output still goes through Last Touch
   (or Video Last Touch) and Chief of Staff.** Nothing spends real money.
   Every Stripe payment link or product page is a public sales action, so
   it needs Chief of Staff approval like an outreach email.
