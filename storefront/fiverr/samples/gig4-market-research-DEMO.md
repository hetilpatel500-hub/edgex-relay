# DEMO SAMPLE: Competitor snapshot

> **Portfolio sample. Not client work.** The "client" (Hearthloop) is a
> fictional startup made up by Edgex. The competitors below are real
> companies, and every fact about them is from public pages found by web
> search on 2026-09-25, linked next to each claim. Prices change often;
> treat them as a snapshot on that date. Written with AI assistance and
> reviewed before publishing.

**Brief (invented for the demo):** Hearthloop wants to sell refill-at-home
cleaning products to renters in small apartments in the US. Question: who
already sells refills by mail, at what price, and where is the gap?

This is the Basic tier format (3-5 competitors, one table, short
takeaways). Standard and Premium add market sizing, customer-review
themes, SWOT and positioning.

---

## 1. Summary

- The refill-by-mail space is established. At least four US brands sell
  reusable bottles plus refills online.
- They split into two models: **dry tablets / sheets** you dissolve in
  water (Blueland) and **liquid concentrates or cartons** (Branch Basics,
  Cleancult, Grove).
- Entry prices range from about $5 for a single bottle (Cleancult) to
  $75+ for a starter kit (Branch Basics).
- Gap for a new brand: none of the four lead on "small apartment" as the
  core promise (storage space, letterbox-size delivery). That angle is
  open, but it's a positioning gap, not a product gap.

## 3. What each one is missing (the opening for Hearthloop)

- **Blueland:** tablets need a bottle per product, so a full kit still
  takes shelf space. Room to pitch "fewer bottles."
- **Branch Basics:** high entry price ($75+). Room for a cheaper starter.
- **Cleancult:** reviewers note cartons are still single-use packaging
  ([The Zero Waste List](https://thezerowastelist.com/is-cleancult-sustainable/)).
- **Grove:** broad marketplace, not focused on one use case. A narrow
  "small apartment" brand can speak more directly.

## 4. Catch

Four funded brands already own this search space. A newcomer needs a sharp
angle and a low-cost way to reach renters. This snapshot doesn't size the
market or test demand; the Standard tier would.

## 5. Method

Web search of each brand's own site and independent reviews on
2026-09-25. No paid databases. No surveys. Nothing here is a guess; where
a number wasn't found, the table says so.
